Summary

Across seven auctions where a conventional Government of India comparator could be matched, India's sovereign green bonds priced an average of 2.0 basis points below the conventional curve. The programme has therefore lowered the government's borrowing cost, but by an amount worth roughly 29 crore rupees a year against an interest bill of 9.28 lakh crore. The more consequential finding is how that pricing was held: between May 2024 and June 2025 the Reserve Bank cancelled two green auctions outright, truncated a third, and let two more devolve on primary dealers, leaving a 10,302 crore rupee shortfall in the year's green borrowing.

Key findings
  1. Mean and median greenium of +2.0 basis points across seven matched observations, and +1.75 bps once weighted by the amount actually placed with investors.
  2. The greenium reached zero in late 2023. The 5-year auction of 10 November and the 10-year auction of 8 December both priced at exactly the interpolated conventional yield.
  3. The two largest 10-year greeniums came with the worst auction outcomes: 28 per cent of the August 2024 issue sold, and 70 per cent of the November 2024 issue devolved on primary dealers.
  4. The only clean, fully-placed positive observation was the 30-year on 13 December 2024, at +4.7 bps. Tenor, not the label, is the variable that separates success from failure.
  5. In FY2024-25 less than a fifth of notified 10-year green issuance reached investors. All of the 30-year did.
  6. Proceeds went overwhelmingly to clean transportation, and a single line item, electric locomotive production at 9,928.99 crore rupees, is 48 per cent of the FY2023-24 programme.
  7. There is no evidence of additionality, and the framework never required it. In both reported years the government spent more on eligible schemes than the bonds raised and funded the excess from general revenue.
  8. The FY2023-24 allocation report contradicts itself: its chart shows renewable energy at 6,116.3 crore rupees, its detailed table 4,578.47 crore.

Where the greenium went

In November and December 2023, in back-to-back auctions, the 5-year and 10-year green bonds both priced at exactly the interpolated conventional yield. Zero basis points. Twice. By the second year of the programme, the green label was worth zero on paper the market actually wanted to buy in size. Then it got worse and better at the same time. The two largest 10-year greeniums I measured, 2.0 bps in August 2024 and 3.3 bps in November 2024, were bought at a price. In August the government sold 28 per cent of what it offered. In November it sold 5,000 crore on paper, but 3,498 crore of that devolved on primary dealers.

A cut-off yield is normally the market-clearing price for the full notified amount. When the issuer truncates the quantity, it stops being a clearing price and becomes a reservation price.

Tenor is the variable that matters

The one clean observation is the 30-year. On 13 December 2024 the 6.98% SGrB 2054 sold its full 5,000 crore at 6.98 per cent against an interpolated 7.0267 per cent on the 7.09% GS 2054. That is +4.7 bps, fully placed, no devolvement, no rejection. Insurers and pension funds buying 30-year duration have a genuine scarcity problem, and green paper at that maturity gives them something conventional supply does not. In FY2024-25, less than a fifth of the notified 10-year green issuance reached investors. All of the 30-year did.

What it is worth, and what it is not

Take the top of my measured range, 5 bps, and apply it to the 57,697.4 crore raised across FY2022-23 to FY2024-25. That is about 29 crore a year of interest saved, against central government interest payments of 9.28 lakh crore. The green label is saving roughly three-thousandths of one per cent of the interest bill. India's programme has demonstrated that a rupee-denominated sovereign green curve can be built, sold, listed and reported on. It has not demonstrated that investors will pay a premium at scale, and it has not demonstrated additionality, because it was not designed to. The case for continuing is real. It is just not the case that is usually made: the programme is worth running as market infrastructure, not as a cheap funding strategy, because at 2 basis points it is not one.
